Questions to ask

Important legal distinction

Apostille, authentication and legalisation are not interchangeable.

An apostille is a certificate used where the 1961 Convention applies to the relevant public document and route. It generally simplifies the authentication of signatures, seals and capacities, but it does not validate a document's contents or assure acceptance by a receiving authority.

When the Convention does not apply, other authentication or legalisation steps may be necessary. There is no universal sequence applicable to every document or destination.

Source: HCCH Apostille Convention, especially Articles 1–5. Always consult current official sources and the institution that will receive the document.

Common questions

A few questions to ask before a document crosses borders.

Where was the document issued, and where will it be used?

Identify the exact issuing jurisdiction and destination. Treaty application, territorial coverage and objections may matter.

Who is the intended receiving authority?

Contact the destination institution or its official guidance to confirm its document acceptance requirements, including any translation or document-age rules.

Does the Convention apply to this document?

Consult the HCCH status table and treaty scope, including entry into force, exclusions and the relevant relationship between jurisdictions.

Which authority can issue the required certificate?

Find the official competent authority or government department authorized for the relevant document and jurisdiction.

Can DWB Global legalize or certify my document?

No. This website is educational. DWB Global does not provide official apostilles, legalisation, professional licensing or legal advice.
